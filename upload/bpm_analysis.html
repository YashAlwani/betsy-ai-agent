Betsy: Autonomous Procurement Agent

Before & After Business Process Model showing the operational impact of an Agentic AI tool on a small manufacturing procurement workflow. Grounded in the Betsy mock environment (12 SKUs, 6 suppliers, 4 live scenarios).

Project: Betsy — Autonomous Procurement POC Primary Stakeholder: Jenny (Operations Manager) Date: 28 May 2026 Phase: Phase 2 — Core Autonomous Loop

Executive Summary

Operations Manager Jenny currently spends 30–35 hours per week on procurement firefighting — manually checking inventory, chasing supplier quotes by email, drafting purchase orders, following up on deliveries, and reconciling invoices by hand. This reactive, sequential workflow leaves no time for strategic work and creates persistent risk of production-line shutdowns and duplicate payments.

  • The Betsy agent automates ~80% of procurement touchpoints, running the detect → source → order → reconcile cycle continuously and without human initiation.
  • Human review is preserved and meaningful — scoped to high-value decisions, price anomalies, and flagged exceptions — reducing Jenny's procurement load to 2–4 hours per week.
  • Three live mock scenarios demonstrate this concretely: Betsy detected a 1.5-day stockout window for SKU-003 Steel Rods and dispatched a PO to QuickShip Express before any human noticed; identified a $1,470 duplicate invoice (INV-2024-099 matching INV-2024-089) in under 100ms; and correctly escalated a 50% price spike on Steel Rods rather than auto-purchasing.

Key AS-IS Pain Points
P3 — Reactive Detection
Stockouts identified only during Jenny's periodic manual checks. SKU-003 Steel Rods reached 45 units (1.5 days of production cover) before being noticed. Reorder point: 200 units.
P5 — 48-Hour Sourcing Bottleneck
Email-based RFQ with 3–4 suppliers takes 1–2 business days. Not all 6 suppliers are contacted every cycle. QuickShip Express (1-day lead — best for urgent orders) often missed.
P9 — Approval Chain Latency
Manager sign-off via email adds 1–2 days. Weekends, holidays, and meetings extend this further — compounding the stockout risk window for critical SKUs.
P12 — Invoice Errors Undetected
Manual duplicate detection fails at scale. INV-2024-089 and INV-2024-091 are identical $1,470 invoices from MidRange Supply (SUP-006) for 10× Circuit Board Type A — both in the system with no flag.

Key TO-BE Process Gains
P3 — Proactive Detection
Betsy calculates days_remaining = current_stock / daily_usage_avg continuously. SKU-003: 45 ÷ 30 = 1.5 days. PO triggered immediately — no waiting until Monday's manual check.
P4–P5 — Instant Parallel Sourcing
All 6 suppliers queried simultaneously from the catalog in under 1 second. QuickShip Express (SUP-003, 1-day lead, 0.92 reliability) automatically selected for the urgent SKU-003 stockout.
P7 — Smart Escalation Only
Price spike scenario: SKU-003 best quote ($13.60) is 19.3% above historical avg ($11.40). At the 30% threshold, normal orders auto-approve; anomalies route to Jenny with full context and reasoning.

Step-by-Step Process Comparison
Process Activity AS-IS — Manual (Before) TO-BE — Agentic AI (After) Impact
Inventory Monitoring
 Jenny manually checks ERP spreadsheet once or twice a week. Stockouts often identified with fewer than 2 days of cover remaining. Betsy polls /api/inventory continuously. Calculates days_remaining = current_stock ÷ daily_usage_avg for all 12 SKUs. Threshold alert fires instantly. Reactive weekly → proactive real-time. SKU-003 example: 1.5 days remaining detected immediately vs. noticed Monday morning after the fact.
Supplier Sourcing
 Email 3–4 known suppliers. Wait 1–2 business days for replies. Not all 6 suppliers are contacted every cycle. Quotes compared manually in spreadsheet. Betsy queries all 6 suppliers via /api/suppliers simultaneously. Filters by availability: true and catalog SKU match. Total time: under 1 second. 48-hour sourcing cycle → under 1 second. All 6 suppliers evaluated every time. Best-scoring option selected, not just whoever replies first.
Supplier Selection
 Manual comparison based on experience and last known price. Reliability history not systematically tracked or weighted. Multi-factor scoring across unit_price, lead_days, and reliability_score. For urgency: QuickShip (0.92 score, 1-day lead) beats BulkSupply (0.88, 7-day) despite higher price. Gut-based → evidence-based. Supplier reliability_score updates after each transaction outcome (learning loop). Decision rationale logged for audit.
Purchase Order Creation
 Jenny drafts PO manually in ERP. Sends manager an email for sign-off. Waits 1–2 days. Then manually emails PO to supplier. Entire cycle: 2–3 days. Betsy POSTs to /api/purchase-orders with full reasoning. Status set to "pending_approval" for anomalies; auto-transitions to "approved" for routine orders. PO dispatched immediately. 2–3 day cycle → minutes for routine orders. Manager approval preserved for high-value/anomaly via push notification. Zero lag on standard reorders.
Delivery Tracking
 Jenny calls or emails supplier to check status. Estimated 5–8 hours per week of follow-up. No systematic monitoring; surprises on Monday mornings. Betsy monitors expected_delivery from PO record. PATCHes /api/purchase-orders/{po_id}/status on confirmation. Proactive late-delivery alert sent to Jenny if window is missed. 5–8 hrs/week chasing → zero manual effort. Proactive alerts replace reactive Monday morning discoveries.
Invoice Reconciliation
 Finance manually matches invoice to PO in spreadsheet. Ad hoc duplicate check. INV-2024-089 and INV-2024-091 — both $1,470 from SUP-006 — paid without a flag being raised. Betsy runs _find_duplicates() on every invoice submission: matches supplier_id, total_amount, and date proximity (<60 days). HIGH RISK if under 30 days apart. Manual / error-prone → automatic, 100% coverage. INV-2024-099 flagged immediately as duplicate of INV-2024-089 (same $1,470, SUP-006, 29 days — HIGH RISK).
Price Anomaly Detection
 No systematic baseline comparison. Jenny may sense "this seems high" but has no formal threshold. Risk of overpaying during supply disruptions under production pressure. Betsy compares current quote against stored historical_avg_price. If deviation exceeds the 30% spike threshold, escalates to Jenny with all quotes, history, and recommendation rather than auto-ordering. No guardrail → enforced 30% threshold. Price spike scenario ($13.60 vs $11.40 avg) correctly triggers flag_for_approval action before any PO is issued.
Decision Audit Trail
 Ad hoc — email chains and some ERP entries. No structured log of why supplier X was chosen over Y. Finance cannot trace decision rationale for any given PO. Every decision logged to /api/agent-log: timestamp, SKU, candidate suppliers with scores, chosen supplier, reasoning, and eventual outcome. Human-readable and queryable. No systematic record → full transparency. Satisfies finance audit requirements. Enables bias review. Every PO traceable to its exact scoring inputs.

3
Wait 24–48 hrs for replies With only 1.5 days of stock, even QuickShip's 1-day lead is dangerously close. Approval chain adds another day. Production line at risk
AFTER (Betsy)

Continuous monitor detects breach Betsy sees SKU-003 at 45 units vs reorder_point of 200. days_remaining = 45 ÷ 30 = 1.5. Alert fires immediately — no human needed. <1 min detection
2
All 6 suppliers queried, QuickShip selected Only QuickShip Express (SUP-003) offers 1-day lead for SKU-003. Selected automatically by scoring. PO generated. <5 sec sourcing
3
PO dispatched, Jenny notified PO sent immediately. Stock arrives next day — 0.5 days buffer before shutdown. Jenny receives dashboard update. Expected supplier: QuickShip Express

Flagged before payment duplicate_flags array returned in API response. Invoice held for Jenny review. Payment blocked. $1,470 protected
